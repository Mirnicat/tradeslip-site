Security

Last updated: 20 September 2026

Tradeslip holds your clients' names, addresses, phone numbers and what you charged them. That is real data about real people, so here is exactly how it is handled - and how to tell us if something is wrong.

Where your data lives

  • Everything is stored in the app's private sandbox on your device - a SQLite database and a media folder that no other app on the phone can read.
  • There is no server. We operate no database, no account system and no cloud storage for your records, so there is nothing of yours for us to lose, hand over or have breached.
  • The database is deliberately kept out of shared folders such as Documents, and Android's own backup agent is switched off for it (allowBackup=false, plus explicit data-extraction rules), so your client list is not copied into a backup you did not choose.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of it. See deleting your data.

What the app sends over the network

  • Until you open the subscription screen, the app makes no network connection at all. The billing library is not even started.
  • Once you open it, the only traffic is to Google Play and to RevenueCat, to check whether you have an active subscription. No estimate, invoice, client record, photo or PDF ever leaves your device through the app.
  • Sending a PDF uses your own phone's share sheet. You choose the app and the recipient; Tradeslip hands the file to the app you picked and nothing else.
  • There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK and no advertising ID. The app does not build a profile of you.
  • Network connections that are made are pinned to HTTPS with the system trust store only, so a user-installed certificate cannot silently intercept them.

Permissions

The installed app asks for no runtime permissions at all - there is no permission dialog to accept, ever. It does not ask for storage, contacts, location, camera or photo-library access. Adding a logo or a job photo uses the Android system photo picker, which hands the app the one image you chose and nothing more, which is why no gallery permission is needed.

For completeness, the entries that do appear in the app's manifest are:

EntryWhy
INTERNETRequired by the billing library to check your subscription. Nothing else in the app uses the network.
ACCESS_NETWORK_STATESame library, to tell "offline" apart from "failed".
com.android.vending.BILLINGGoogle Play billing.
USE_BIOMETRICOnly so the optional fingerprint unlock can be offered. Android performs the check; the app is told yes or no and never sees anything about your fingerprint or face.
USE_FINGERPRINTThe same thing on Android 7 and 8, where the newer entry did not exist yet.

All five are normal-level entries: Android grants them at install and never prompts you. None of them can read anything of yours.

On-device protection

  • App lock. An optional PIN, asked for every time the app returns to the front. The PIN itself is never stored - only a salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash of it, compared in constant time. Repeated wrong attempts trigger an increasing delay, which always expires.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock, optional, as a shortcut past the PIN. Android does the checking; Tradeslip is told only yes or no. It is deliberately biometric-only: it will not fall back to the phone's own PIN or pattern, because that would mean anyone who can open the handset can open the client list.
  • A recovery code. Because the PIN genuinely cannot be reset by us, setting one issues a single 16-character recovery code, shown once. It is stored the same way the PIN is - as a salted hash, never readable - and entering it removes the lock without touching a single record. There is no other back door, for anyone.
  • Screenshot blocking. An optional setting that marks the app's window secure, so it is excluded from screenshots, screen recording and the recent-apps preview.
  • Encrypted backups. A backup file contains every client you have. You can seal it with a password; it is then encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key derived by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256. GCM authenticates as well as encrypts, so a file that has been altered is refused rather than partly decrypted.
  • Export hygiene. CSV exports neutralise leading =, +, - and @ so a value in your data cannot be turned into a formula when the file is opened in a spreadsheet.
  • Restore safety. A restore shows you what is in the file - how many documents, clients and payments, and when it was made - before anything is replaced, and it runs as a single transaction. A restored file can never set or change the app lock, so a backup cannot be used to lock you out of your own phone.

Build integrity

  • Release builds are code-shrunk and obfuscated, with native debug symbols stripped and uploaded separately.
  • Values that must survive obfuscation - anything written into the database - are stored as explicit strings, never as a class or field name that a rename could change. An app update can never silently corrupt records that way.
  • The app contains no secret keys. The only key compiled in is the public, client-side billing key, which is designed to be readable.
  • Releases are signed with a key held offline by the developer.

Reporting a vulnerability

If you believe you have found a security problem in Tradeslip, please tell us before telling anyone else, through the support page, with "SECURITY" in the subject. Include what you found and how to reproduce it.

We will acknowledge a report within 5 working days and keep you informed until it is resolved. We will not take legal action over good-faith research that stays within your own device and your own data, does not disrupt other users, and gives us reasonable time to fix the issue before it is published. We are happy to credit you.

What we would tell you about

We hold no personal data of yours on any system we run, so a breach of ours cannot expose your client list. If a defect in the app were ever found to put data on your own device at risk, we would publish it on this page and in the release notes, and ship a fix as quickly as we could.
